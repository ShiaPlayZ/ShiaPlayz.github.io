The dish

The taco is a significantly easy dish to make. More on that later though. It's filled with all the right ingredients such as any meat you want and/or any vegetables. (having meat is optional.) The taco shell is a crunchy and delicious part of the taco but mostly not usually talked about, since the toppings and insides are way more in their variety and every toping is customisable!


The recipe (Standard)

Ingredients Equipment
Ground beef (or chicken/beans) Skillet or frying pan
Taco seasoning Wooden spoon or spatula
Taco shells (hard or soft tortillas) Chef's knife
Shredded lettuce Cutting board
Diced tomatoes Small prep bowls
Shredded cheese (Cheddar or Monterey Jack) Cheese grater
Sour cream Measuring spoons
Salsa or hot sauce Tongs (for warming tortillas)

Steps

  1. Brown the meat: Heat your skillet over medium-high heat, add the ground beef, and break it apart with your spatula until fully cooked.
  2. Drain excess grease: Carefully tilt the skillet to pour out any excess fat from the meat.
  3. Season the meat: Add the taco seasoning and a splash of water, then simmer according to the package instructions until the sauce thickens.
  4. Prep the toppings: Use your chef's knife and cutting board to shred the lettuce and dice the tomatoes, placing them into small prep bowls.
  5. Grate the cheese: Use the cheese grater to shred your Cheddar or Monterey Jack cheese if you bought a block.
  6. Warm the tortillas: Use tongs to briefly heat your hard shells in the oven or warm your soft tortillas in a dry skillet.
  7. Assemble the tacos: Spoon the seasoned meat into the center of each tortilla.
  8. Add the toppings: Layer on the cheese, lettuce, tomatoes, sour cream, and salsa to your liking.

The History

The taco originated in pre-colonial Mexico as a nutritious staple of soft corn tortillas filled with fish and organs, but it evolved significantly over the centuries into the global comfort food known today. The name "taco" itself is relatively modern, tracing back to 18th-century Mexican silver miners who likened the food to the paper-wrapped gunpowder charges they used in the mines. Mexican immigrants introduced the portable street food to the United States in 1905 through Los Angeles food carts, and by 1920, the recipe adapted to American palates by substituting organs with ground beef and adding cheddar cheese, lettuce, and sour cream. To streamline operations and mass-produce the dish, fast-food giants like Taco Bell later adopted the pre-fried, U-shaped crunchy shell, which was originally patented by Mexican cooks in the 1940s. While modern fast-casual chains now experiment with global fusion flavors like Korean bulgogi, contemporary dining trends show a massive resurgence in authentic taquerias that reject Americanized ingredients in favor of traditional meats, soft corn tortillas, cilantro, and fresh radishes. Taco Tuesday

'Taco Tuesday' is a time of year where buissnesses start selling tacos every Tuesday. Taco Tuesday was created way back in 1993. Taco Tuesday is mostly celebrated in the US but has also reached other english speaking countries like the UK and Australia. Another, similar, Taco Tuesday get held in Norway called Fredagsmys where they serve tacos on friday (usually at night.) Many states in the US have trademarked the phrase 'Taco Tuesday', but have all been relinquished! :)
